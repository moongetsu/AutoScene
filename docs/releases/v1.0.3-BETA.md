AutoScene 1.0.3 Beta exports clips bit-perfect, knows 3D animation as its own
kind of footage, and has a Fast Scan that finds faces up to about three times
as fast, at the cost of finding fewer. Exports on an NVIDIA card now keep their
preset's quality.

## What's new

- **Bit-Perfect exports.** A new format, **Bit-Perfect (lossless)**: every
  frame of a clip decodes to exactly the pixels of the episode, in its own
  size, pixel format, colour and HDR, and the sound is the episode's own
  samples. Each clip is decoded again after it is made and compared frame by
  frame; a clip that isn't exact isn't kept.
  - Pictures are stored as lossless HEVC, which After Effects shows exactly
    like the usual H.264 and HEVC episodes (8 and 10 bits). On an NVIDIA card
    most SDR footage is stored several times faster; HDR, 4:2:2 and 12-bit
    pictures go through the slower software encoder, which keeps their HDR
    metadata.
  - The rare pictures HEVC can't hold as they are (an odd width, or an odd
    height in 4:2:0; NTSC DV's 4:1:1 colour; alpha; more than 12 bits) become
    FFV1 in an MKV, and the dialog says so: DaVinci Resolve and VLC open
    those, After Effects doesn't.
  - Files are big: about 45 to 50 GB an hour of clean 1080p, twice that or
    more with film grain. The size shown before the export is a middle
    figure between the two.
  - A scenepack becomes one file when its episodes are alike; when their
    picture, colour or sound differ, every shot gets a file of its own, and
    the dialog says why. Episodes that differ only in their HDR metadata
    can't be joined yet: export those with a file per shot.
- **3D animation.** A project's footage can now be **Animation**
  (computer-animated film). Its characters are grouped in a space fitted to
  the film, so characters that look alike to a face model trained on people
  are told apart. When the analysis runs on a graphics card (Windows and
  macOS, not with Fast Scan), faces are also searched at up to three sizes
  (79 % of the faces found instead of 72 % on a test film). Choose it when you
  add a project, or in **Edit details…** (which replaces **Rename…**);
  resting the pointer on an option shows what it means.
- **Fast Scan** (**Settings → General**, off by default). Projects created
  while it is on find their faces with smaller face models and a few frames
  of each shot: about three times as fast on a 4K film when an NVIDIA card
  decodes it, up to about one and a half times otherwise. It finds fewer
  faces, so less of each character's screen time ends up on them (on our
  tests 78 % instead of 96 % in live action, 59 % instead of 94 % in a 3D
  film), and no one is found by their body. Switch a project in **Edit
  details…**.
- **NVIDIA exports keep their quality.** H.264 and HEVC exports on an NVIDIA
  card hit a bitrate cap that left all-intra presets, and grainy footage with
  a GOP, short of their preset's quality. They now keep it, so those files
  are bigger: an all-intra pack about two and a half times (1.60 GB became
  4.41 GB; a cleaner one 1.06 GB became 2.46 GB).
- **Better size estimates.** The size shown before an export now counts the
  picture size and sound the export writes, and the encoder of an NVIDIA card.
  All-intra presets came within about 15 % on NVIDIA (three test packs) and a
  few percent with ProRes; with a GOP the footage decides more. AMD, Intel and
  Apple encoders are still counted like the software ones.
- **Time left you can trust.** The time left is measured for each step of a
  job, from its first move, instead of over the whole job.
- **Names stay with their person.** Naming or hiding a character now pins the
  sharpest of its typical faces (the ones most like the rest of it) instead of
  its sharpest faces, which could belong to someone else grouped with it.
  Characters you named before keep the faces pinned then: if someone else's
  face came along, mark it "not them".
- **Long names fit better.** A long character name in the character's title
  first gets smaller, down to four fifths of its size, before it is cut off.
- **Playing backwards** (J) keeps its speed on recordings with a varying
  frame rate, and a frame from before a seek no longer moves the playhead
  back.
- **Sturdier and safer, after an outside audit.**
  - A decision you made on a face (who it is, "not them", "not a face", a
    chosen picture) is no longer lost when a new analysis doesn't find that
    face, for example after switching Fast Scan or the footage type. It waits,
    is tried again at every later analysis, and comes back once one finds
    that face again (for example after switching back). The cast page says
    how many wait.
  - Removing an episode or a project waits for its work to stop, and what a
    crash left behind is cleared when a project opens. A big import in one
    project no longer holds up another project's work, and an analysis step
    that stopped unexpectedly shows **Retry** instead of "analysing".
  - Relinking a moved file checks its picture and is saved in one step. A
    clip of a shortened episode fails on its own, not the whole export, and
    an export never replaces a file that appeared at its name meanwhile.
  - Model downloads can't hang, check for free space first, and models are
    checked again before use. **Settings → About** lists every model with its
    licence: the full scan's face and people models may only be used for
    non-commercial work, Fast Scan's (MIT, Apache-2.0) for any work. On
    Windows and macOS an analysis the graphics card can't take says it runs
    on the CPU.
  - The installed app runs only its own FFmpeg and takes only plain file
    paths from its window. A downloaded "video" that is really a playlist is
    refused at import (it could make the app open other files, a network
    share's included). Deleting a project with its files removes its folder
    only when that folder's own database says it is that project. The log
    names an episode by its file name instead of its full path.
  - A single very long file (a whole season in one file) is grouped into
    characters within a fixed amount of memory, instead of crashing the app on
    a small machine at every start.

## Before you install

- **Already installed?** The app updates itself (**Settings → Updates**).
  Your projects and models are kept.
- **Your cast is grouped again once.** On its first start, this version
  groups the characters of every project again (**Grouping characters** in
  the activity list). Names, characters you made and your decisions stay.
  Live-action casts come out as before, or very nearly. A project of an
  animated film you made as **Live action** or **Mixed** is grouped like
  animation now and can come out quite differently: set it to **Animation**
  in **Edit details…** to have its faces searched at three sizes too.
- **Switching a project to or from Animation or Fast Scan** finds all its
  faces again, episode by episode, and its characters fill up again as that
  goes; your names and decisions are kept.
- **Exports on an NVIDIA card get bigger.** Their H.264 and HEVC presets now
  get the quality they ask for: all-intra files come out about two and a half
  times as big as before, and grainy footage with a GOP bigger too.
- **Not signed by a publisher yet,** so the first start needs your OK once.
  On Windows, SmartScreen asks first: choose **More info → Run anyway** (with
  Smart App Control on, Windows 11 blocks the installer without that choice).
  On macOS, drag AutoScene to Applications, open it and close the message
  that macOS can't verify it. Then open **System Settings → Privacy &
  Security**, scroll down to the note about AutoScene, choose **Open Anyway**
  and confirm. (Since macOS 15, right-click → Open no longer does this.) If
  macOS later refuses a part of the app, such as `libonnxruntime.dylib`, run
  `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/AutoScene.app` once.

## Check your download

Each file comes with a signed record (a build attestation) of the workflow
of [tcmmi/AutoScene](https://github.com/tcmmi/AutoScene) that built it, and
the tag and commit it was built from. To check one, use the
[GitHub CLI](https://cli.github.com), signed in with any GitHub account:

```bash
gh attestation verify AutoScene_1.0.3-BETA_x64-setup.exe -R tcmmi/AutoScene
```

To insist on this tag and the release workflow, add
`--source-ref refs/tags/v1.0.3-BETA --signer-workflow tcmmi/AutoScene/.github/workflows/release.yml`.

Or compare SHA-256 hashes: put `SHA256SUMS` next to your downloads and run
`sha256sum --check --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS` (Linux) or
`shasum -a 256 --check --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS` (macOS). In PowerShell
on Windows, this prints `True` for an intact download:

```powershell
(Get-FileHash .\AutoScene_1.0.3-BETA_x64-setup.exe).Hash -eq (-split (Select-String -Path SHA256SUMS -Pattern x64-setup.exe -SimpleMatch).Line)[0]
```
